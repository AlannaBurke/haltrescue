A hamster's enclosure is its entire world. Unlike dogs or cats who roam freely, a hamster spends virtually all of its life within the walls of its cage — burrowing, foraging, running, and sleeping. Getting the housing right is the single most impactful welfare decision you will make as an owner.

## Why Size Matters More Than You Think

Wild hamsters travel up to **5 miles per night** in search of food [1]. While a captive hamster does not need to replicate this exactly, the instinct to move, explore, and burrow is deeply ingrained. Enclosures that are too small cause a well-documented stress response called **stereotypic behaviour** — repetitive, purposeless movements such as bar-chewing, pacing, and repeated climbing. These are not personality quirks; they are signs of chronic stress [2].

The **US minimum is 600 square inches** of uninterrupted floor space, but this is a floor, not a target. Research consistently shows that hamsters in larger enclosures show fewer stereotypies and spend more time in natural behaviours [2]. **We recommend 800+ square inches for all species**, and **775+ square inches as a true minimum for Syrian hamsters**, who are the largest domesticated species.

| Species | Minimum (US) | HALT Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Syrian hamster | 600 sq in | 775+ sq in |
| Campbell's / Winter White dwarf | 600 sq in | 800+ sq in |
| Roborovski dwarf | 600 sq in | 800+ sq in |
| Chinese hamster | 600 sq in | 800+ sq in |

## Recommended Enclosures

The following enclosures are widely used and recommended by the hamster care community. All meet or exceed the 600 sq in minimum, and most comfortably exceed the 800 sq in recommendation.

### Wooden & Modular Cages

**Niteangel Vista / Bigger World M/L** (800–1,000 sq in) — A popular wooden-framed cage with a large glass front panel, excellent ventilation, and deep bedding capacity. The M and L sizes both exceed the Syrian minimum comfortably.

**Pawhut** (1,070 sq in) — A wooden modular cage with multiple levels and ramps. Spacious and aesthetically pleasing, though the multi-level design means some floor space is occupied by ramps.

**GDLF Hamster Cage** (775 sq in) — A white cabinet-style cage that meets the Syrian minimum. Good ventilation and a clean look, though on the smaller end for Syrians.

**Bucatstate 2.0 / 3.0** (775–1,109 sq in) — Available in two sizes. The 3.0 (120×60 cm) is an excellent choice at 1,109 sq in. Features a distinctive porthole window.

**Prolee 40"×20"** (800 sq in) — A wooden-framed glass terrarium style cage. Excellent for deep bedding and observation.

**Frisco Oasis** (1,109 sq in) — A dark wood cabinet cage on wheels. Large, sturdy, and easy to move.

### Aquarium Tanks

Glass aquarium tanks are a classic and highly functional option. They hold deep bedding well, prevent bedding scatter, and allow clear observation. They must always be used with a **secure mesh lid** to provide ventilation.

- **75 Gallon Tank** — 864 sq in
- **40 Gallon Breeder** — 648 sq in (meets minimum; suitable for dwarfs and as a minimum for Syrians)
- **120 Gallon Terrarium (120×60 cm)** — 1,109 sq in

### DIY IKEA Builds

IKEA furniture can be converted into excellent hamster enclosures. Popular options include the **Platsa**, **Pax**, **Linnmon**, and **Komplement** units. DIY builds allow full customisation of size and layout, and can be significantly more affordable than purpose-built cages. Mesh panels or acrylic sheets are used to replace solid sides for ventilation. Many tutorials are available in the hamster care community.

### What to Avoid

**Small pet store cages** — The brightly coloured plastic cages sold in most pet stores (Habitrail, CritterTrail, etc.) are almost universally too small. Many provide fewer than 200 square inches of floor space. These cause stereotypic behaviour and should not be used [2].

**Multi-level wire cages** — Cages with multiple wire mesh levels (like the popular Ferplast Duna or similar) are problematic. Wire mesh floors and ramps can injure hamster feet, and the vertical space does not substitute for horizontal floor area. Hamsters do not naturally climb; they burrow.

**Bar spacing too wide** — If using a wire cage, bar spacing must be **½ inch or less** for Syrian hamsters and **¼ inch or less** for dwarf hamsters to prevent escapes and head entrapment.

## Bedding and Burrowing Depth

Burrowing is one of the most important natural behaviours for hamsters. In the wild, hamsters construct complex multi-chamber burrow systems for sleeping, food storage, and safety [1]. Denying this behaviour causes significant stress.

Provide **at least 6–10 inches of bedding** across the entire enclosure floor. The deeper the better — many experienced keepers provide 12 inches or more.

**Safe bedding options:**
- Paper-based bedding (Kaytee Clean & Cozy, Carefresh, etc.)
- Aspen shavings
- Orchard grass or meadow hay mixed in for nesting material

**Never use:**
- Cedar or pine shavings — the aromatic phenol oils are toxic to small animals and cause respiratory damage [3]
- Scented or deodorised bedding
- Fluffy cotton or kapok "nesting fluff" — fibres can wrap around limbs and cause amputations, and are fatal if ingested [4]

## Essential Enrichment

A large, well-bedded enclosure is the foundation, but enrichment fills it with purpose.

**Wheel** — Non-negotiable. Use a **solid-surface wheel** (no mesh or bars, which cause foot injuries and tail entrapment). Syrian hamsters need a **10–12 inch wheel**; dwarf hamsters need a **6.5–8 inch wheel**. A wheel that is too small forces the hamster to run with an arched spine, causing chronic pain and spinal deformity over time [5].

**Sand bath** — A shallow dish of **heat-treated children's play sand or reptile sand** (not dust, not calcium sand) is essential for grooming. Hamsters roll in sand to remove excess oils from their coat. This should be a permanent fixture, not an occasional treat.

**Hides** — Provide multiple hides of different materials: wooden houses, ceramic hides, cork bark, and cardboard tubes. Hamsters are prey animals and need to feel concealed to feel safe.

**Foraging** — Scatter-feeding pellets and seeds into the bedding instead of using a bowl encourages natural foraging behaviour and provides mental stimulation.

**Chews** — Apple wood sticks, willow balls, and untreated cardboard keep teeth trim and provide enrichment.

## Environment

Keep the enclosure in a room maintained between **65–75°F (18–24°C)**. Temperatures below 60°F can trigger torpor (a hibernation-like state that can be fatal in domestic hamsters), and temperatures above 80°F risk heatstroke [6].

Place the cage in a **quiet area** away from direct sunlight, drafts, televisions, and speakers. Hamsters are crepuscular to nocturnal and need undisturbed rest during the day. Constant noise and light disruption are significant stressors.
